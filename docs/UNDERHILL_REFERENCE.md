## Teaching decisions

Underhill emphasizes awareness of lips, tongue, jaw and voice. WordSpeak uses
those four controls to help the learner notice a small physical change, then
apply it in a word. The activity sequence and artwork are original adaptations.

His descriptions of vowel exploration and moving between s/sh support teaching
movement between sound references. A learner can now select Find, Move, Feel
and Use, manually explore an illustration, or replay it. The visual is silent:
the learner tries the sounds; official demonstrations are linked for a human model.

The default practice now shows one cue and a single recording/check action.
The longer exploration is optional. This is a product-design choice intended to
reduce effort, not a measured claim about student motivation or effectiveness.
The target reference stays visible, including a front lip view. Unrounded does
not mean equally spread: artwork controls now distinguish spreading from rounding
and jaw opening. Gentle sideways arrows make the seat lip cue explicit; sit
relaxes that spread, while food uses rounding. These are qualitative examples,
not requirements for identical facial geometry across learners or accents.
Each target has an optional activity and a self-check using listening, a mirror,
a hand near the mouth, or gentle awareness of jaw/throat movement. The p exercise
includes breath release. The t/k activities are WordSpeak applications of contact
and release principles, not copies of separate Underhill t/k lessons.

## Ten targets

| Target | Main exploration |
|---|---|
| /iː/ | From sit: a slight lift and forward shift |
| /ɪ/ | From seat: relax lips and shift slightly inward/down |
| /ɛ/ | From bad: raise jaw and tongue a little |
| /æ/ | From bed: lower jaw and tongue a little |
| /uː/ | Explore lip rounding from a front-vowel position |
| /p/ | Lip closure and brief release into pen |
| /t/ | Front tongue contact and release into ten |
| /k/ | Back tongue contact and release into key |
| /s/ | Explore a forward shift from sh |
| /ʃ/ | Explore a backward shift from s |

The bed vowel is often written /e/ in British teaching materials; this project
retains /ɛ/. Positions vary by accent, especially the food vowel. These activities
do not impose one exact mouth configuration on every English speaker.

## Boundaries and validation

The teaching example is explicitly separate from the model's observed-category
hypothesis. No learner anatomy, vowel duration or learning gain is measured.
Recognition code and thresholds are unchanged. Functional checks do not establish
that the adapted activities improve students' pronunciation. Instructional content
still benefits from review by the project's pronunciation teacher.

Original artwork is used; no Sound Foundations chart or video is copied into
the app. Attribution does not imply review or endorsement by Adrian Underhill.
