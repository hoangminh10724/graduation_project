## Run the learner journey

In the configured workspace, start WordSpeak with `START_WORDSPEAK.cmd` and open [the sound lab](http://127.0.0.1:8013/demo). For a clean checkout, see the setup below. Choose **seat**. Explore /i/, mime the movement, say the word, and replay it. Select **Save word for conversation**. Rehearse “Is this seat free?” Select **Reply** and ask someone on a bus whether you can sit beside them. Record your own wording. If a partner is present, select **Ask a listener**, hand over the device, and have them replay the latest turn and enter what they understood. Compare their paraphrase with your intention. Select **Clarify** and try a different phrasing for a listener who missed the question. Replay both turns and decide which one makes your intention easier to understand.

**Practice this sound** returns to the same word for a focused adjustment. **Phrases & conversation** also opens the saved-word list on a later visit. **Add your own word or expression** lets a learner bring in vocabulary from outside the catalog, with an optional rehearsal sentence and a question from their own conversation. For example, enter *rain check*, “Could I take a rain check?” and “Can you come to dinner tonight?” The same reply, clarification, recording and listener checks apply. Sound diagnosis is available for supported catalog prompts. **Practice saved words** starts with an item with no recorded reply, then the least recently practised one. **Next saved word** excludes the current item. The browser-local bank preserves older catalog saves, personal wording, completed reply-recording counts and the last reply time. Recording a reply measures participation only. The two latest clips for each word stay in the tab until reload. A saved word records a learner’s choice to practise it. It provides no evidence of mastery.

## Use the chart to build speech

Under the word selector, open **Tap the sounds**. Build *seat* as /s/ → /i/ → /t/, undo a tap if needed, then say the word in one movement. Choose **I can go.** and try it once with /ə/ and once with /æ/ in *can*. Compare the meaning you would give each reading. The activity contains reference sequences for all 51 words and three short phrases. The 41 keys are grouped by movement, with an approximate vowel map and vertical pairs for stops and fricatives. Choosing a different sequence offers comparison and retry. Tapping checks a symbolic sequence and supplies no acoustic grade. This is an original General American adaptation of Underhill’s chart-based visual dictation. ## Listen and respond

In Sounds, open **Listen for a sound difference**. Choose one of twelve contrasts, listen to a short sentence, then select the word you heard. **Replay line** repeats the same item. The app reveals the spoken word and one physical cue after the listener chooses. This checks recognition of one device-generated example. It supplies no score for the learner's own speech.

In Phrases & conversation, **Listen to line** speaks the current practice sentence or partner question, including learner-added situations. **Hide line while listening** allows an audio-first reply. Slower playback is optional. Both activities use a US English voice installed on the learner's device. When that voice is unavailable, the app explains the limitation and leaves recording, text and partner practice available. The existing comparison recordings have unverified redistribution permissions and are outside the listening library.

## Review the phrasing of a recorded reply

After each completed recording in **Phrases & conversation**, show **Recording assessment** under that take's player. For fixed exercises, the browser submits the clip to `/transcribe`, locates the practice line and compares an acoustic cue with the selected variant. Feedback describes relative loudness, ending pitch movement or a quiet boundary. For conversation takes it submits the WAV and the saved expression to `/tutor/observe`. A supported sound receives a provisional comparison and replay position. A missing word, ambiguous measurement, poor capture or unreachable service produces explanatory feedback. The learner can retry a failed request using the existing take. This check is separate from sending a signed-in dialogue turn.

For either phrase or conversation recordings, open **Explore pitch in this recording**. The local worker estimates fundamental frequency in periodic frames and draws it over the recording’s timeline. The learner can replay an intended rise or fall and compare it with a second attempt. Frames outside 65–600 Hz, clipped or quiet frames and frames with weak periodicity can leave gaps. The estimator uses a normalized difference function inspired by YIN. The paper's performance figures do not transfer to this implementation. Intonation appropriateness and vocal anatomy are not inferred. [de Cheveigné and Kawahara, 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12002874/)

Signal checks covered five harmonic tones from 80 to 550 Hz, a rising tone, silence, constant input, clipping and noise. A simulated microphone carrying a 200 Hz tone also exercised the recording, on-device worker and browser display. Playback stops when a new recording begins.

Choose **Send for transcription** on a recorded turn. When the response includes usable word times, **Listen to your phrasing** groups the recognized words at gaps of at least 0.5 seconds. Select a group to replay the original recording from just before its first word. The learner can compare grouping across two attempts and decide where a pause helped communicate the message. The cutoff is a navigation choice. A gap can include unrecognized speech. Older responses with only text, or invalid time spans, still allow ordinary playback.

The browser check exercised timestamp-based seeking in a recorded clip. A separate live check joined two validation recordings with one second of additional silence. The endpoint returned a 2.14-second gap between recognized words, including the recordings’ original boundary silence and alignment uncertainty. It also substituted words in the second sentence. These timings cannot establish a fluency score or whether a particular pause was appropriate.

### Evaluate adjudicated sounds in recorded words

The user can supply the recordings and ratings in their existing format. For analysis, map each adjudicated target occurrence to one JSONL row with a unique `id`, pseudonymous `speaker`, `audio` path relative to the manifest, whole-word `start` and `end` in seconds, catalog `prompt_id`, and expert `verdict` of `acceptable`, `difference` or `unclear`. For a difference, include the actual `heard_phone` in IPA or the literal `deletion`. Use complete word boundaries on the source timeline. Keep acceptable variants under the expert's verdict and retain unresolved disagreements as unclear. Include all sampled occurrences, including words on which the app issues no warning. A file with only known errors cannot estimate real-world warning precision.
